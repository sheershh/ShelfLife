Less guessing, less waste

ShelfLife started as a college mini-project with one question: why do good households throw away good food?

The problem

Most food that is wasted is thrown out at home, and a lot of it is thrown out because people aren't sure. Is a spotty banana still fine? Does spinach last longer in a box or a bag? When the answer isn't clear, the safe-feeling choice is the bin, and the water, land and energy that grew the food go with it.

What ShelfLife does

  • Freshness scanner. Take a photo and get a ripeness reading, a mould check and one clear recommendation.
  • Food guide. Shelf-life estimates for the room, fridge, pantry and freezer, plus storage tips and spoilage signs.
  • Use it up. Simple ideas for food that is past its prime but still good to cook with.
  • Your shelf. Save the foods you buy often and look back at your earlier scans.

How the scanner works

Your photo is shrunk in your browser and sent to our server, which asks Google's Gemini vision model to describe what it sees in a fixed format: the food, its ripeness, any mould and a freshness score. Our server then checks that answer, applies its own safety rule (visible mould on soft food always means "throw it out") and adds storage advice from the ShelfLife food guide. The model reads the photo; the advice comes from our own data.

What it can't do

ShelfLife judges visible quality, not safety. A photo can't show smell, temperature history or bacteria, and food can be unsafe without looking spoiled. Use the scanner and guide alongside the label and your own senses. When in doubt, throw it out.

Built with

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript in the browser; Node.js and Express on the server; MongoDB Atlas for data; Google Gemini for image understanding. ShelfLife supports UN Sustainable Development Goals 12 and 2.
